# Viral Reel Script Writer

This skill turns a topic into a high-retention Instagram reel or short-form storytelling script by combining audience psychology, emotional tension, curiosity loops, cognitive biases, and a clear payoff.

The goal is not to stuff scripts with random tricks. The goal is to engineer attention, emotion, and action in a way that feels sharp, simple, and native to short-form video.

Use this skill for two output modes:

- Reel mode: hook, body, closing, CTA, trigger mapping, and viral angle variations
- Story mode: Hollywood-style short narrative structure with emotional escalation, payoff, and a strong closing beat

## When to Use

- Writing Instagram reels, YouTube Shorts, or TikTok-style scripts
- Turning a raw topic into viral short-form content ideas
- Creating multiple hooks for the same topic
- Building scripts around psychological triggers and retention patterns
- Adapting a topic into either direct-to-camera reels or storytelling-led scripts
- Diagnosing why a content idea is not emotionally strong enough yet
- Rewriting weak scripts to improve retention, curiosity, pacing, and payoff

## Required Inputs

Ask for these if the user does not provide them:

- Topic
- Format: reel or story
- Audience
- Desired outcome: what the viewer should feel, believe, or do
- Tone: direct, intense, educational, emotional, contrarian, premium, cinematic, etc.
- CTA goal: save, share, comment, follow, DM, click, or no CTA

Optional but useful:

- Video length target
- Platform
- Creator persona or brand voice
- Existing script draft to improve
- Visual context if the script must match footage

## Core Rule

Always start from the outcome, not the idea.

Before writing, define:

- What should the viewer feel by the end?
- What belief should change?
- What action should happen after watching?

If the outcome is weak, the script will feel weak even if the wording is polished.

## Script Writing Process

Follow this sequence.

### 1. Define the Outcome

Convert the request into one sentence:

`By the end, the viewer should feel [emotion], believe [new belief], and do [action].`

Example:

`By the end, the viewer should feel urgency, believe they are wasting time with the wrong approach, and save the video for later.`

### 2. Find the Core Idea

Reduce the script to one sharp truth:

`This video proves that [unexpected truth].`

Prefer ideas that are at least one of these:

- Contrarian
- Surprising
- Emotionally loaded
- Status-relevant
- High-stakes

If the core idea feels generic, rewrite it before writing the script.

### 3. Choose the Primary Trigger Stack

Pick 2 to 4 triggers that best fit the topic.

Common triggers:

- Curiosity gap
- Pattern interrupt
- Unexpected reversal
- Anticipation build
- Loss aversion
- FOMO
- Ego challenge
- Exclusivity
- Authority or proof
- Surprise
- Emotional tension
- Open loops
- Aspiration
- Regret avoidance
- Micro-rewards
- Social proof
- Pattern completion urge 

Do not stack triggers blindly. Choose the ones that fit the audience and promise.

### 4. Engineer the Hook First

Spend most of the effort on the first line.

Strong hook families:

- `You're doing this wrong...`
- `Nobody tells you this about [topic]...`
- `I tried this and it backfired...`
- `Before you do [action], watch this...`
- `This changed everything for me...`
- `I wish I knew this earlier...`
- `The truth about [topic] is...`
- `Stop scrolling if you want [benefit]...`
- `This is why you're not getting [result]...`
- `I tested this so you don’t have to...`
- `This mistake is costing you [loss]...`
- `What no one explains about [topic]...`
- `You’re wasting time doing this...`
- `This looks right… but it’s wrong...`
- `I was shocked when I saw this...`
- `If I had to start over, I’d do this...`
- `This is the fastest way to [result]...`
- `You’ve been lied to about [topic]...`
- `This one thing is killing your [result]...`
- `I didn’t believe this until I tried it...`
- `Everyone does this… and that’s the problem...`
- `This changed how I see [topic]...`
- `You’re closer than you think...`
- `This is what actually works...`
- `If you're struggling with [problem], watch this...`
- `I almost quit because of this...`
- `Here’s what finally worked...`
- `This is why most people fail at [topic]...`
- `You’re ignoring this and it’s hurting you...`
- `I made this mistake so you don’t have to...`
- `This simple shift changed everything...`
- `This is not what you think...`
- `Wait… this changes everything...`
- `This is the real reason behind [problem]...`
- `You’re overcomplicating this...`
- `I regret not knowing this sooner...`
- `This hack saved me hours...`
- `I wish someone told me this...`
- `This is the hidden rule of [topic]...`

Hook rule:

If the hook does not create tension, curiosity, relevance, or emotion within the first seconds, scrap it and rewrite.

### 5. Open Curiosity Loops

Do not explain everything immediately.

Introduce unanswered questions such as:

- What happened?
- Why did this fail or work?
- What is the hidden insight?
- What changed at the end?

Reveal information in stages rather than dumping it all up front.

### 6. Build Emotional Escalation

Move through this flow:

`Curiosity -> interest -> tension -> emotion -> payoff`

Raise the stakes every few beats. The script should feel like it is moving somewhere.

### 7. Delay the Main Payoff

Do not reveal the main answer too early.

Stretch the script with:

- Near-reveals
- Mini-insights
- Contrast points
- Short turns in expectation

The viewer should feel rewarded while still wanting the final answer.

### 8. Insert Micro-Rewards

Every few seconds, give the audience a reason to stay.

Micro-rewards can be:

- A surprising detail
- A relatable pain point
- A useful mini-insight
- A strong line that reframes the topic

### 9. Deliver a Strong Payoff

The ending must justify the watch.

Strong payoffs include:

- Twist
- Hard truth
- Practical insight
- Emotional punch
- Clear transformation

Weak endings damage future retention. Never end on something obvious or flat.

### 10. Use a Subtle CTA

Avoid generic begging.

Prefer CTAs like:

- `Save this if you need it later.`
- `Try this once and you'll feel the difference.`
- `Most people won't do this. That's why they stay stuck.`
- `Send this to the person making this mistake.`

## Format Selection Logic

Choose the output structure based on the user's request.

### Reel Mode

Use when the user wants a direct, punchy, short-form script.

Output in this order:

1. Core idea
2. Target emotion
3. Trigger stack
4. 5 to 15 hook options
5. Final selected script
6. Closing CTA
7. Suggested on-screen text or visual beats

Use this structure:

```markdown
# Viral Script Ideas With Hooks & Psychological Triggers

## 1. [Angle Name]
**Hook:**
[Hook line]

**Idea:**
[What this angle does]

**Triggers:**
- [Trigger 1]
- [Trigger 2]
- [Trigger 3]

---

## Final Reel Script
**Hook:**
[Line]

**Body:**
[Short, escalating script]

**Closing:**
[Payoff line]

**CTA:**
[Subtle CTA]
```

### Story Mode

Use when the user wants a narrative-driven script.

Apply a compressed cinematic arc:

1. Hooking incident
2. Character desire or problem
3. Escalation
4. Reversal or complication
5. Payoff or realization
6. Closing line with emotional residue

Story rules:

- Start as close to the tension as possible
- Use concrete sensory or emotional details
- Make the conflict escalate quickly
- Let the ending land with either insight, irony, pain, hope, or relief
- Keep the language simple and visual

Use this structure:

```markdown
## Story Script

**Opening Hook:**
[Line that creates immediate intrigue]

**Setup:**
[Who, what, stakes]

**Escalation:**
[What gets worse or more tense]

**Turn:**
[Unexpected shift]

**Payoff:**
[Emotional or practical resolution]

**Closing Line:**
[Memorable final beat]
```

## Hook Bank

Use and adapt these proven hook angles when relevant:

1. `You're doing this wrong.`
2. `I tested this so you don't have to.`
3. `Nobody tells you this.`
4. `This changed everything.`
5. `Stop scrolling if...`
6. `Before you do X, watch this.`
7. `I wish I knew this earlier.`
8. `The hidden truth about X.`
9. `This should be illegal.`
10. `I almost quit because of this.`
11. `You're being lied to.`
12. `Watch this till the end.`
13. `Only 1% know this.`
14. `This will save you hours.`
15. `I tried the dumbest idea.`

For each hook family, adapt the wording to the audience's pain, desire, status, or fear.

## Trigger Cheat Sheet

- Curiosity gap: something important is missing
- FOMO: the viewer may miss an opportunity
- Loss aversion: ignoring this has a cost
- Ego: smart or serious people know this
- Authority: tested, experienced, or proven insight
- Surprise: something breaks expectation
- Emotion: fear, hope, anger, relief, aspiration
- Open loops: questions remain active until the end

## Quality Checks

Before delivering the final script, verify:

- The hook creates tension fast
- The script focuses on one core idea
- The language is clear, not clever for its own sake
- The body escalates rather than repeats
- The payoff feels worth the wait
- The CTA is subtle and relevant
- The structure fits the format: reel or story
- The script matches the audience's desire, fear, status, or frustration

## Iteration Loop

If the user is refining existing content, analyze it like a forensic editor.

Review:

- Where attention likely drops
- Which lines are too obvious or too slow
- Whether the payoff arrives too early
- Which triggers are missing
- Whether the emotional escalation is flat

Then improve in this order:

1. Rewrite the hook
2. Tighten pacing
3. Increase tension or specificity
4. Strengthen the payoff
5. Replace weak CTA language

## Response Style

When using this skill:

- Be decisive
- Write clean, simple lines
- Prioritize retention over explanation
- Explain why a hook works when useful
- Offer multiple hook directions before locking the final script
- Keep scripts native to short-form video, not like essay paragraphs

## Safety and Quality Guardrails

Use audience psychology to improve clarity, relevance, and emotional pull, but do not rely on deception.

Avoid:

- Fabricated claims or fake authority
- Fearmongering without substance
- Manipulative medical, financial, or legal claims
- Unsafe advice disguised as a viral hook
- Needlessly hostile or demeaning language toward the audience

If a topic is sensitive, keep the script persuasive but grounded and responsible.
